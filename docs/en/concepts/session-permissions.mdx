A session opened through a delegation can be limited, or extended, without creating a single role. For every capability of the site you choose one of four permissions.

## The four permissions

| Permission | In the session |
|---|---|
| **Not set** | Nothing changes: the account keeps what its roles give it. Every capability starts here |
| **Allow** | The capability is granted everywhere in the session, unless a role prohibits it |
| **Prevent** | The capability is taken from the roles of the account on the whole site, as Prevent does in **Define roles**. An override of a role in a course or an activity still applies |
| **Prohibit** | The capability is refused everywhere in the session, and nothing can grant it back |

## Site and delegation

The setting of a delegation wins over the one of the site, and a delegation set to Not set follows the site. So the site can forbid what no delegation should ever do, and one delegation can still be tighter or wider.

## What they do not touch

- They apply only to sessions opened through a delegation. No role is created or changed, and the owner of the account keeps every permission when logging in normally.
- The access data of the session is rewritten on every request, so a change reaches open sessions from their next page.
- Site administrator accounts have every capability whatever is set. This is one more reason to keep **Protect site administrator accounts** enabled.
- Capabilities that Moodle checks with database queries instead of the session (for instance some lists of users or courses) are not affected.

> [!WARNING]
> Allow gives a capability to a session even when the account does not have it. Changing session permissions needs `local/delegateaccount:managepermissions`, which no role has by default, and a person can only allow capabilities they hold themselves.

To set them, follow [the guide](page:guides/session-permissions).
